To beat it

Shoot an arm until it breaks, then the other. Each broken arm removes its attacks, but the boss gets angrier. When both are gone the core opens and the boss switches to teleport slams. Keep moving, and change direction after an attack locks on. The boss punishes habits, not reactions.

The fight

Three phases, set by what you break

The boss's phase depends only on its own damage. Each phase changes its temperament: how long it rests, how much risk it accepts and which attacks it has left.

Measured

phase 1
Both arms intact
  • Attacks: cannon, slash, double rear teleport
  • Normal cooldowns
  • Rests 1.15 s after each attack
  • Risk weight 1.0: cautious under fire

Enraged

phase 2
One arm destroyed
  • Loses the broken arm's attacks
  • Cooldowns × 0.8
  • Rests 0.85 s
  • Risk weight 0.6: prefers combos

Desperate

phase 3
Both arms gone, core open
  • Only attack: core teleport and slam
  • Slam cooldown 7 s → 5.5 s
  • Rests 0.85 s
  • Risk weight 0.3: presses you hard

Breaking an arm plays a 1.4 s break animation. Opening the core takes 2 s, and the core can only be damaged after it opens.

The AI

How it thinks, every 0.2 seconds

The original boss checked distance, intact arms and ready cooldowns, then picked from a fixed order: blade, then teleport, then cannon. The adaptive boss stacks five layers on top of the same attack state machine. Each layer answers one question.

One decision, in plain words

  1. See. You are visible at 7 m, moving left at 3.4 m/s. The player model has seen you strafe left after the last four cannon bursts.
  2. Set the mood. Both arms are intact and you have 90% health, so the phase is Measured and the director is at normal (lead factor 0.65).
  3. Ask the learner. At mid range the cannon has hit you 6 times out of 9. A Thompson draw gives cannon 0.71, double teleport 0.44.
  4. Plan. Expectimax looks two moves ahead: "Cannon, then you most likely strafe left and stay at mid range, then the double teleport is ready." That line scores highest.
  5. Act. It starts the cannon burst. Each of the three shots leads your path by up to 1.7 m.
  6. Learn. Each shot is recorded as a hit or miss. Your movement between lock and impact becomes one more dodge observation.

Layer by layer

The five layers

The tags show where each idea comes from: the SE3062 lectures, or game AI beyond them.

1
L03 model-based agentL09 probability

Player model RiftPlayerModel

How does this player move, and how do they answer each attack?

The boss keeps an internal state about you, updated every 0.1 s while you are visible. This is what makes it a model-based agent rather than a reflex agent.

velocity v̂ ← v̂ + 0.35 · (Δp/Δt − v̂) exponential smoothing range r̄ ← r̄ + 0.05 · (distance − r̄) strafe bias b ← EMA of sign(lateral velocity) −1 left … +1 right answer P(r | attack) = (n_r + 1) / (N + 6) Dirichlet(1,…,1) prior

Dodge answers

Between each attack's telegraph and its impact, your movement is classified as one of six answers: Stay, Strafe left, Strafe right, Back off, Close in, Jump. Counts are kept per attack.

Why the +1

The Dirichlet prior is Laplace smoothing. With no data every answer has 1/6. After 4 strafes left out of 4 cannon bursts, strafe left is (4+1)/(4+6) = 50%, not 100%. One observation never makes the boss certain.

It also tracks which part you are shooting (damage over the last 10 s) and when Relay heals you.

2
Beyond: target leading

Predictive aim RiftWardenBrain.Lead

Where will the player be when the shot lands?

The original boss aimed at where you were at the lock. Constant movement made you untouchable. Now the cannon and slam aim at a constant-velocity intercept, deliberately weakened so that changing direction still works.

aim = p + v̂ · t · κ, |v̂ · t · κ| ≤ 2.5 m t: 0.17 / 0.47 / 0.77 s after lock (cannon), up to 0.6 s (slam) κ: lead factor 0.4 (mercy), 0.65 (normal), 0.85 (pressure)

Because κ is below 1 and the lead is capped, a player who runs in a straight line gets hit, and a player who reacts to the lock escapes. Try it in the aim explainer.

3
L09 Bayes' ruleL03 learning agentBeyond: Thompson sampling bandit

Attack learner RiftAttackLearner · BetaBelief

Which attacks actually work against this player, at this range?

Each attack has a separate belief for each range band (close < 4 m, mid 4–10 m, far > 10 m). The belief is a Beta(α, β) distribution over its hit chance, starting at Beta(2, 2): a weak 50/50.

hit α ← γ·α + 1 miss β ← γ·β + 1 γ = 0.97, floor 1 mean α / (α + β) pick draw θ ~ Beta(α, β) for each ready attack, use the draws

Bayes

The Beta is the conjugate prior of a hit/miss outcome, so Bayes' rule reduces to adding 1 to a count. Each cannon shot and each melee or slam swing is one trial.

Explore vs exploit

Thompson sampling draws a plausible hit chance from each belief. An untested attack has a wide belief, so it sometimes draws high and gets tried. A proven attack usually wins. Forgetting (γ) fades old evidence, so the boss notices when you change tactics.

4
L10 adversarial search: expectimaxL03 expected utility

Combo planner RiftComboPlanner · Expectimax

If I do this, how will they react, and what punishes that?

A depth-2 expectimax search over a small fight state: range band, cooldowns, phase and weakest part. The boss's moves are MAX nodes. Your answers are CHANCE nodes, weighted by the player model. Hit chances come from the learner.

MAX boss move: Cannon · Slash · Double teleport · Approach · Hold CHANCE your answer r ~ P(r | move) hit ~ θ(move, band) MAX boss follow-up in the new state value = hit chance × payoff − w_risk · extra damage taken risk = rate × duration × (exposure − 1) exposure: teleport 0.5, approach 0.8, slash 0.9, cannon/hold 1.0 leaf = ½ × best attack ready soon, × 0.85 per second of waiting

Why expectimax, not minimax

Minimax assumes you always pick the reply that is worst for the boss, which makes it timid. You are not a perfect opponent. Expectimax weights your observed replies, so the boss exploits your habits.

Why relative risk

You are shooting the boss whatever it does, so standing still is never safe. A move is charged only for the extra damage it invites. Under heavy fire the rear teleport earns a bonus, because you have to turn around.

The learner joins at the root: each attack's value is shifted by (Thompson draw − posterior mean) × payoff, so exploration survives planning. The whole tree is at most 6⁴ leaves and runs in well under a millisecond. Try it in the planner explainer.

5
L03 hierarchical state machineL03 utilityBeyond: dynamic difficulty

Phases, tactics and director RiftDirector

Given my damage and how the fight is going, how should I behave?

The phase (Measured, Enraged, Desperate) sets the parameters the attack states run with: a hierarchical state machine. Three utility tactics run while it pursues you:

Shield the weak arm

An arm below 50% health that you keep shooting is turned up to 25° away from you.

Punish the healer

If Relay healed you in the last 6 s and is in sight and range, a cannon burst may target Relay. At most once per 12 s.

Cut off

While approaching, it walks to a point 2 m to the side you usually strafe toward, not straight at you.

The director is covered in Fairness rules.

Smarter, not cheaper

Against every moving style, the hit rate rose 12–21 points. The Kiter's more than doubled, because the old boss kept firing where the kiter had been.

Defence practice

Viva questions

Short answers you should be able to give from memory.

What kind of agent is the Rift Warden?

A layered, model-based, learning agent in a competitive multi-agent environment. It keeps an internal model of the player (model-based), updates its beliefs from outcomes (learning), and chooses by expected value over a short search (utility-based).

Describe its task environment.

Partially observable (you can break line of sight), multi-agent and competitive, stochastic (your dodges), sequential, dynamic and continuous, with known rules. What is unknown is your behaviour, and learning it during the fight is the point.

Why a Beta distribution for hit chance?

A hit is a Bernoulli outcome, and the Beta is its conjugate prior. Bayes' rule becomes counting: a hit adds 1 to α, a miss adds 1 to β. The mean is α/(α+β), and the width shows how sure the boss is.

Why Thompson sampling instead of always using the best mean?

Always using the best mean never tests the alternatives, so the boss could get stuck on an attack that worked early. Sampling from each belief tries uncertain attacks in proportion to the chance they are actually best. That balances exploration and exploitation.

What does the forgetting factor γ = 0.97 do?

Before each update α and β shrink by 3% (never below 1). Old evidence fades, so the problem is treated as non-stationary: if you change tactics, the boss's beliefs follow within a few trials.

Why expectimax and not minimax?

Minimax assumes the player always picks the reply that is worst for the boss. Real players are not optimal, so minimax would make the boss overly cautious. Expectimax weights replies by their observed probability, which lets it exploit habits. The library has a pessimistic mode, and a unit test shows a tree where the two disagree.

Why is risk relative to standing still?

The first version charged absolute risk. Because the boss is shot whatever it does, every move looked costly and it froze while under fire; a regression scenario caught it. Charging only the extra damage a move invites, (exposure − 1), fixed it.

How do you stop predictive aim from being unfair?

The lead factor is below 1 (0.4–0.85) and capped at 2.5 m, so changing direction after the lock still dodges. Telegraphs and damage never change. The director adds a mercy window below 25% health.

Why the +1 in the dodge probabilities?

It is a Dirichlet(1,…,1) prior, which is Laplace smoothing. With few observations no answer gets probability 0 or 1, so one dodge does not make the boss certain.

How did you show it is better than the original?

Scripted archetypes fought both versions under identical conditions. Hit rate rose 12–21 points against all moving styles. Against fixed habits variety fell, which is the bandit exploiting. The 43 unit tests check the algorithms against hand-solved cases, and the regression harness proved the refactor kept the original behaviour.

What would you improve next?

Test against human players who change tactics, since the scripted ones cannot show the forgetting at work. Separate dodge tables per range band. A deeper search with pruning if more attacks are added.
